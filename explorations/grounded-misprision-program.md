# Grounded Misprision: A Research Program

Nov 20, 2026 · @Sean Butner

## Summary

Language grows a shared world model only when readings of signs are tested against the world. Both branches of misprision, and the split between human expansion and machine collapse, follow from that one condition.

The two existing Lean files defined creative and destructive misprision, then assumed growth. This program derives them instead. It adds a world, sensoria that sample it, and readers who test readings and re-read stored forms as context changes.

Four claims carry the program:

1. **Misprision is unavoidable.** Any rate-limited code read in diverging contexts misreads with positive probability.
2. **Its sign is set by selection, not by the misreading.** A reading that is tested against evidence independent of the utterance raises expected accuracy; an untested one drifts toward the reader's prior.
3. **Expansion needs an independent channel.** A reader of a fixed corpus adds grounded information only through evidence that is not itself a function of the corpus. Human readers have that channel; a model trained on its own outputs does not.
4. **Open-endedness needs expressive power.** Unbounded growth requires a message space that can state unbounded hypotheses, false ones included, and instructions the hearer can execute. Animal signaling systems found so far fall short of this, though some are compositional.

Status: proposal only, no proofs. The conjecture table ranks each claim by difficulty and names what would falsify it.

## Primitives

The model has eight objects. The key move is that a hearer does not decode a message into a state; it builds a candidate meaning, scores it against its own evidence, and keeps the form so it can re-read it later.

1. **World.** A probability space (Ω, P) with a state process ω\_t. A group G acts on Ω and leaves P invariant: time shifts, rotations, the cycle of seasons.
2. **Sensoria.** Each agent a observes O\_{a,t} = σ\_a(ω\_t, ξ\_{a,t}) through its own channels: perceptual, bodily, social. Its private evidence E\_a is everything it observed.
3. **Agent state.** s\_a = (b\_a, S\_a, κ\_a): a belief b\_a over a hypothesis space H, a store S\_a of retained forms with their sources, and a context κ\_a built from E\_a and S\_a.
4. **Language.** A message space U generated by a grammar Γ. A form u has no fixed content. Its meaning is an instruction: run in context κ, it builds a hypothesis h ∈ H.
5. **Speaking.** A speaker draws u from π\_s(u | b\_s, κ\_s) under a channel rate of R bits per second.
6. **Reading.** A hearer l builds a distribution over hypotheses ρ\_l(h | u, κ\_l). Misprision is any gap between ρ\_l and the speaker's intended meaning m\_s(h | u).
7. **Evaluation and re-evaluation.** The hearer weighs u as evidence through a model of the speaker (trust τ) and scores h against E\_l. When κ\_l changes, every stored form is re-read: ρ\_l(h | u, κ\_{l,t'}).
8. **Archive and machine reader.** An archive copies forms with fidelity φ per copy. A machine reader sees only a corpus D: it fits q ≈ P\_D and writes by sampling q.

Two measures are tracked. Grounded information is the mutual information between the world and the collective state:

```latex
\mathcal{G}_t \;=\; I\big(\Omega \,;\, W_t\big), \qquad W_t = (b_{a,t},\, S_{a,t})_{a \in A}
```

Explicit content is separate. It is what agents can state, as opposed to what follows from what they jointly hold. The gap between them is the room for deduction to add content without adding grounded information.

## Core theorems: misprision as consequence

Three results replace the old definitions. Misprision is forced by the channel; communication alone cannot add grounded information; and whether a misreading helps or harms depends on how it is selected, not on the misreading itself.

### T1. Misprision is unavoidable

If the meanings a speaker wants to convey carry more information than the channel rate R (A3), rate–distortion theory forces expected distortion D(R) > 0. If two hearers read a polysemous form in different contexts (A4), their readings differ for some form. Either way, the probability that ρ\_l differs from m\_s is positive. Misprision is a property of every narrow, context-read code, not a defect of some.

### T2. The closed loop cannot gain

Suppose the collective updates by talk alone, W\_{t+1} = F(W\_t, ξ\_t), where the noise ξ\_t is independent of Ω given W\_t. Then Ω → W\_t → W\_{t+1} is a Markov chain, and the data-processing inequality gives:

```latex
I(\Omega;\, W_{t+1}) \;\le\; I(\Omega;\, W_t)
```

Every "Bloomian growth without observation" in the old Lean files is this case. New content in a closed loop is either deduction (explicit content rises while 𝒢 stays flat) or conjecture with no support yet.

### T3. The bifurcation follows from selection

A reading yields a candidate h. The hearer keeps it according to a test on evidence E′ that is independent of u given ω: fresh observation, or testimony from someone with separate access. Let L be the expected log score on future observations. The effect of integrating one reading splits into three terms:

```latex
\mathbb{E}[\Delta L] \;=\; \underbrace{\Delta_{\text{pool}}}_{\ge 0} \;+\; \underbrace{\Delta_{\text{select}}}_{\ge 0} \;-\; \underbrace{\Delta_{\text{drift}}}_{\ge 0}
```

- **Pool.** The reading treated as evidence, combined with the hearer's own. Faithful readings, and creative readings that are really inferences, land here.
- **Select.** Conjectures kept because they passed a test on independent data. Like held-out validation, this cannot hurt in expectation, and it grows with the power of the test.
- **Drift.** Readings kept for reasons unrelated to the world: trust without testing, conformity, prestige, fluency. Untested chains regress toward the reader's prior, which is the Griffiths–Kalish result for Bayesian iterated learning.

Creative misprision is the regime where pool plus select exceed drift; destructive misprision is the reverse. The misreading is the same act on both branches. The conjecture is a threshold: a critical ratio λ\* of test power to untested retention, above which 𝒢 grows in expectation and below which it decays.

&#91;embedded content: misprision bifurcation · one test, two branches, one re-reading loop\]

A reading that passes an independent test raises grounded information; one kept untested drifts toward the prior. A reading that fails is set aside, but its form stays in the store and is re-read when the context changes.

Vigilance (A5) supplies the select term. Re-reading (A6) lets a failed reading be tried again in a later context, so selection compounds over time; this is the hermeneutic circle as a sequence of tests.

Early evidence fits the select term. In LLM self-training over 10 generations, compositionality first rose and then fell without filtering; only task-grounded filtering sustained it, and random filtering did not ([Guo et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23054)).

One literary corollary is worth stating and testing: on this account Bloom's "strong" misreadings are the ones that survived selection, so the canon is a record of tested readings.

## Expansion versus collapse

The difference is the wiring, not the substrate. A human reader's commentary depends on the world through evidence that bypasses the corpus. A self-trained model's output depends on the world only through the corpus.

```latex
\text{machine: } \Omega \to D \to q \to D' \qquad\qquad \text{human: } \Omega \to D,\;\; \Omega \to E_i,\;\; (D, E_i) \to C_i
```

### T4. Grounding dichotomy

Let readers write commentary C\_i on a finite corpus D.

1. **Independent evidence.** If C\_i = f\_i(D, E\_i) and each E\_i is independent of D given ω, then I(Ω; D, C\_1, …, C\_n) ≥ I(Ω; D). The inequality is strict whenever some E\_i says something about Ω that D does not. Gains shrink as readers' evidence overlaps.
2. **Closed loop.** If C = f(D, ξ) with ξ independent of Ω, then keeping D and C together gains nothing: I(Ω; D, C) = I(Ω; D). Replacing D with C loses: I(Ω; C) ≤ I(Ω; D), strictly under finite sampling, which drops the tails first.
3. **Deduction.** With no new evidence, a reader who can run the instructions in D (A7, A9) still raises explicit content. 𝒢 stays flat, but more of it becomes usable. T7 says this never runs out.

Part 2 recovers both machine-learning results. If data are replaced, test error grows linearly with iterations; if they accumulate, error stays under a fixed bound ([Gerstgrasser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)). Accumulation stops the loss but, by T2, cannot produce gain.

| Condition | Human reader | Self-trained model | Model with a verifier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence independent of the corpus | Yes: senses, other people, action | No | Within the verifier's domain: execution, proof checking, experiment |
| Test before retention | Vigilance (A5) | None; fits its own samples | Verifier score |
| Keeps forms and re-reads | Yes, given an archive | Only if data accumulate | Yes |
| What the loop fits | The world | Its own output distribution | The verifier |
| Predicted outcome | Expansion | Collapse if data are replaced, a plateau if they accumulate | Expansion inside the verifier's domain, a plateau outside it |

**Sharp predictions.**

- **Strong collapse should vanish under grounded filtering.** One analysis finds collapse persists whenever the synthetic fraction of training data does not shrink to zero ([Dohmatob et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04840)). Here that harm is the drift term. If synthetic data pass a test on independent evidence, they move into the select term, and the persistent harm should disappear. Random filtering of equal strength should not have this effect.
- **Humans in a closed loop should behave like the machine.** Interpretive communities whose evidence is itself derived from the corpus should drift toward shared priors. Commentary traditions tied to an empirical practice (law with courts, medicine with patients) should expand faster than those without one.
- **The human edge is conditional.** Strip a human reader of independent evidence and vigilance, and T4 says it loses its advantage.

## Expressive power: human and animal codes

Compositionality is not the dividing line. What divides is whether the code can state unbounded hypotheses, including false and absent ones, and carry procedures the hearer runs. Below that line, accumulation in the signal system saturates; above it, reading becomes undecidable and commentary never runs out.

| Class | Example | Message space | Displacement | States conjectures | Hearer runs procedures | Growth ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Fixed repertoire | Vervet alarm calls (Seyfarth, Cheney & Marler 1980) | A few calls | No | No | No | Saturates almost at once |
| 2. Parametric | Honeybee waggle dance (von Frisch) | Continuous, fixed dimensions | Yes | Only within one schema | No | Bounded by the schema |
| 3. Combinatorial, finite | Campbell's monkeys (Ouattara et al. 2009); Japanese tits (Suzuki et al. 2016); bonobos ([Berthet et al. 2025](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/190948)) | Finite combinations, some nontrivially compositional | Limited | Limited | No | Finite, so saturates |
| 4. Recursive generative | Spoken human language | Unbounded | Yes | Yes | Short procedures, limited by working memory | Open in H; bounded by memory |
| 5. Generative plus external memory | Writing, notation, code | Unbounded and durable | Yes | Yes | Any procedure | Open; never exhausted (T7) |

The bonobo work matters here: it reports that compositionality is a prominent feature of the bonobo vocal system. The program predicts that this alone does not support open-ended accumulation in the signals.

### T5. Finite codes saturate

If readings range over a finite H (classes 1–3), any monotone accumulation reaches a fixed point within |H| steps per agent. Creative misprision can only swap one existing meaning for another. Any cumulative culture such a species has must be carried by behavior or artifacts, not by the signals.

A corollary links Hockett's design features. To state a conjecture, a code must be able to state something not currently believed: prevarication plus displacement. The capacity to misread creatively and the capacity to conjecture are the same capacity.

### T6. A fidelity threshold

Borrow Eigen's error threshold. With copy error ε per unit and selective advantage σ for the tested content, the largest body of content K that persists satisfies:

```latex
K \;\lesssim\; \frac{\ln \sigma}{\varepsilon_{\text{eff}}}, \qquad \varepsilon_{\text{eff}} \text{ falling with redundancy } n \text{ and archive fidelity } \varphi
```

Writing cuts ε by orders of magnitude, so K jumps. Redundancy across agents does the same through repair by majority. Henrich's demographic threshold becomes a special case in which population size stands in for redundancy. That case is contested: [Vaesen et al. 2016](https://www.pubmed.gov/27044082/) argue the models need implausible conditions. A theorem that separates the role of n from φ and test power would bear directly on that dispute.

### T7. Universal readers never exhaust a corpus

If the interpreter is universal (A9), the set of claims derivable from a finite corpus is recursively enumerable but in general undecidable (Church, Turing). No finite stage of commentary exhausts the corpus, and whether a given claim will ever be drawn out cannot be computed from the corpus alone. For finite codes (T5) the question is decidable. On this account, the human–animal line is the line between decidable and undecidable reading.

**What would falsify this section:** an animal code with unbounded combinatorics and displaced reference whose signals still fail to accumulate; or a finite-repertoire code that sustains cumulative content in the signals themselves.

## Anchored geometry

Reference is what puts world structure into word statistics. If signs co-occur because what they stand for co-occurs, then the world's symmetries reach the statistics, and from there the geometry of learned representations.

### T8. Equivariant reference transfers symmetry

Let a reference map send signs to features of Ω. Assume the world process is G-invariant (A1) and speakers mention things in proportion to how they co-occur in experience (A14). Then sign co-occurrence is G-invariant:

```latex
M(g\cdot i,\; g\cdot j) \;=\; M(i, j) \quad \text{for all } g \in G
```

The learned geometry is then fixed by the harmonic analysis of G. That is the Karkada et al. result for translations, extended to arbitrary finite and compact groups by [Storan, Tolias & Miolane 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.00647). The program adds the upstream step: the symmetry is in the statistics because it is in the world, and reference carries it across.

This gives a precise position on the symbol-grounding debate (Harnad 1990; Bender & Koller 2020). Form alone can recover world structure as a snapshot, because reference shaped the statistics. Form alone cannot refresh that structure, by T2 and T4. Meaning from form is possible as a state and impossible as a process.

### T9. Collapse acts as a low-pass filter (conjecture)

A closed-loop learner estimates the kernel from finite samples each generation. Modes with small eigenvalues have the worst signal-to-noise and should go first. Two predictions follow:

- **Harmonics die in order.** Across self-training generations, the Gram spectrum of month or weekday embeddings should lose higher harmonics in order of eigenvalue. The basic circle survives longest.
- **The world's irregularities die before its symmetries.** Facts that break the symmetry (February is short; seasons flip by hemisphere) should decay first, because closed chains regress toward a smooth prior. Collapse makes representations more symmetric than the world.

The second prediction matches the regularization seen in iterated learning with humans and in LLM self-training. Grounded filtering (T3) should preserve both the harmonics and the irregularities.

### T10. Geometry as a weak internal test

A learned geometry can check readings: a reading in which June follows March breaks the cycle. This gives a model a partial select term without new data. By T2 it is a consistency check against the prior, not new information about the world, so it can slow collapse but cannot reverse it.

## Conjecture space

T3 carries the most weight: almost everything else depends on the selection threshold. The cheapest to test are T9 and the strong-collapse prediction in T4, since both need only self-training runs that groups already perform.

## Test cases

All eleven cases fit the core claim: selection, not content, decides whether a misreading helps or harms. Several force refinements. Meaning becomes the reading process, not its output; positions need a coarse-graining layer; some forms are pointers into an archive; grounded information must be indexed by its target; and speakers can be adversarial. The full list is at the end of this section.

### Homer is still being read

**Verdict: fits (T4 part 1, A6), with one refinement to T7.**

New readings of Homer arrive when readers bring evidence the poem does not contain. Examples: Schliemann's excavations in the 1870s; Parry and Lord's fieldwork with South Slavic singers in the 1930s; Ventris's decipherment of Linear B in 1952; Shay's reading of the Iliad through veterans' combat trauma in Achilles in Vietnam (1994).

The refinement: here inexhaustibility comes from contexts that never stop being new, not from undecidability. T7 should name two sources, contextual and deductive.

- **C16.** Commentary on a classic jumps after independent-evidence shocks and slows between them.
- **Test.** Count Homeric scholarship per year (for example in L'Année philologique) against the dates of those shocks.

### Language changes, yet everything is translatable

**Verdict: fits, with a quantitative twist.**

If every human language is a universal instruction language (classes 4–5), any text in one can be compiled into another. That is effability (Katz 1972), and Jakobson's point (1959) that languages differ in what they must say, not in what they can say. Kolmogorov's invariance theorem bounds the cost: description lengths in two universal languages differ by at most a constant. Language change drifts forms but keeps the class.

- **C17.** Translation loss as a share of a text scales like a constant over its length. It is negligible for long exposition and dominant for short, dense verse.
- **Test.** Measure back-translation loss against length and density. The Piraã debate (Everett 2005) is a live test of whether every language keeps the class.

### The heresy of paraphrase

**Verdict: forces a revision.**

If meaning were the hypothesis h that a reading outputs, a paraphrase producing the same h would lose nothing. Brooks (1947) says it loses the poem. So meaning must be the reading's trajectory, not its endpoint: two procedures with the same output differ if the reader passes through different states on the way (compare Fish 1970).

A literary work is then nearly incompressible relative to any reader's model, though not random. In Gell-Mann and Lloyd's terms (1996) it has high effective complexity: its regularities are themselves long to state.

- **C18.** For literary works, K(work | reader's model) is close to the work's length, so the shortest faithful paraphrase is close to the work itself. Paraphrase loss is T1 applied to an incompressible source.
- **Test.** Compare how well a language model compresses canonical and genre texts, each given a model tuned to its genre. Pair this with human ratings of paraphrase adequacy against paraphrase length.

### Pulp and literature

**Verdict: fits, and gives the sharpest empirical handle.**

Pulp keeps its information in the genre prior, so it reads correctly with ablated attention. Literature keeps its information in long-range dependencies and needs full context. Natural text already shows mutual information between symbols decaying roughly as a power law with distance, which Markov models generically cannot produce ([Lin & Tegmark 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06737v1)). The measurement is contested ([Takahashi & Tanaka-Ishii 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.04848)), so any test must fix the estimator first.

- **C19.** Literature has heavier long-range tails of mutual information than pulp. Truncating a model's context window degrades literature more.
- **C20.** Collapse turns literature into pulp. Closed-loop generation sheds long-range structure first: T9 in the time domain.
- **Test.** Context-ablation curves per genre; the decay exponent of mutual information across self-training generations.

### We comment on everything; commentary forms camps

**Verdict: forces a revision.**

T7 says content is inexhaustible; observation says positions are few. Both hold if positions are a coarse-graining. Readers group readings by agreement, and social drift pulls the groups toward a few attractors. Bounded-confidence models produce exactly this: the number of clusters at equilibrium is roughly 1/(2r), where r is the confidence radius ([survey, 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.05286)).

- **C21.** The number of camps scales with tolerance and with how varied readers' evidence is. Evidence shocks split and reshuffle camps rather than adding positions smoothly.
- **Test.** The Homeric Question: the Analyst and Unitarian camps were recast by Parry's oral theory into oralists and neoanalysts. Track camp structure in citation networks before and after such shocks.

### Thought experiments

**Verdict: fits once tacit and explicit knowledge are separated.**

T2 bounds what the whole state knows about the world, not what the explicit theory knows. A thought experiment moves grounded information already held tacitly, from bodily experience or from accepted laws in separate theories, into explicit theory. Later tests then supply the select term.

Einstein's 1906 box is a clean case. Light carries momentum, so the box recoils; requiring the centre of mass not to move forces the light to carry mass E/c² ([Einstein 1906](https://www.pitt.edu/~jdnorton/teaching/1632_Einstein_Almost_Everyone/notes/Einstein_1906_Emc2.pdf)). Two pieces of knowledge from separate theories combine into a new explicit claim. That is File A's Witness 2 done properly: explicitation, not collective novelty.

A correction to the prompt: the light pulse pushing a box yields mass–energy equivalence, not curvature. Curvature came from the equivalence principle (the freely falling observer, 1907) and the rotating disk (around 1912).

- **C22.** Without observation, the explicit theory's grounded information can rise only up to the tacit grounded information already held. Thought experiments are least reliable in regimes outside lived or measured experience.
- **Test.** Compare how thought experiments in familiar regimes (falling bodies, rigid boxes) and unfamiliar ones (quantum measurement) fared against later experiment.

### Chengyu and memes: pointers into an archive

**Verdict: fits, and adds pointer forms to the model.**

A chengyu is four characters that point to a story in the archive: 守株待兔, the farmer waiting by a stump for another rabbit to break its neck, from the Han Feizi. Its meaning is short only given the archive: K(meaning | shared classics) is small, while K(meaning) is large. When readers lose the story it points to, readings drift toward the literal surface, which is the drift branch.

The reversals show it. 空穴来风 once meant that rumours have a cause, since wind comes through openings; it is now mostly used to mean "baseless". The Contemporary Chinese Dictionary has recorded both meanings since its 6th edition ([Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.com/wiki/%E7%A9%BA%E7%A9%B4%E4%BE%86%E9%A2%A8)). 七月流火, from the Book of Songs, marked the cooling of the year; it is now used for scorching heat.

Memes have the same structure with a fast, shallow archive: a productive template with slots, plus a reference to something recent. Dawkins's original sense (1976) already put them under selection for spread, not for truth.

- **C23.** A pointer form stays readable for as long as its archive persists and is widely shared: centuries for chengyu, months to years for memes. Templates outlive references.
- **C24.** Once the target is out of reach, readings drift toward the literal surface. Reversals happen where the literal reading contradicts the original.
- **Test.** Chengyu usage in historical corpora against rates of classical education; template versus reference survival in dated meme archives.

### Mathematical notation: al-jabr and Euclid against modern symbols

**Verdict: fits, and sits at the opposite pole from the heresy of paraphrase.**

Al-Khwarizmi's al-jabr (around 820) is written entirely in words: "a square and ten roots equal thirty-nine dirhams" is x² + 10x = 39, solved by completing a square drawn as a figure. Euclid states propositions about magnitudes in words, with diagrams. Symbols arrive with Viète (1591) and Descartes (1637).

The theorems survive translation into symbols almost without loss. A theorem's meaning is mostly its output, so paraphrasing it is no heresy. Proofs are different: they are trajectories, and two proofs of one theorem give different understanding.

Notation lowers the cost of running a procedure (A9). Algebra becomes symbol manipulation, and working memory moves onto paper.

- **C25.** Changes in notation raise the rate of new results with no new observation. T7's deductive source becomes cheaper to tap.
- **Test.** Rates of new results before and after symbolic algebra. The usual story of Leibniz's notation and Continental calculus outpacing Newton's in the 18th century is a candidate, but it is contested.

Reading Euclid through symbols is misprision with both branches at once. Tannery, Zeuthen and van der Waerden read Book II as "geometric algebra". Unguru (1975) attacked this as anachronism, and his position is now widely regarded as the accepted orthodoxy among historians ([Utrecht paper, citing Rowe 2012](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/339381/defence.pdf?sequence=1)). As history the algebraic reading misreads the authors; as mathematics it is productive.

That forces a refinement: 𝒢 must be indexed by its target. One reading can be creative with respect to mathematical structure and destructive with respect to the author's world.

### Characters and letters under language change

**Verdict: fits; a script is the archive's choice of what counts as a form.**

A logographic script stores morphemes, so sound change does not break the written form. Classical Chinese stayed readable to trained readers across many centuries, across mutually unintelligible spoken varieties, and in Japan, Korea and Vietnam. An alphabet stores sound. Old texts become hard to read as pronunciation and grammar change, as Old English now needs translation, or spelling freezes and drifts away from speech, as in English.

The trade-off maps onto T6. Characters raise fidelity across time and dialect, but take years to learn, so the premodern literate population n stays small. Alphabets raise n and lower fidelity across time. Each script picks a different point on the same curve.

- **C26.** Character archives sustain longer, deeper commentary traditions on fixed texts, because the form stays legible while readers' contexts drift. Alphabetic cultures retell and modernize more.
- **C27.** Script affects how fast the archive becomes unreadable, not how fast speech changes. Spoken Chinese varieties diverged widely despite a shared script.
- **Test.** Readability against text age for both script types; the ratio of commentary to retelling in each tradition. The 1919 switch to written vernacular in China is a natural experiment: it traded fidelity to the classical archive for a larger n.

### Conspiracy theories

**Verdict: fits strongly; this is model collapse with human readers.**

Conspiracy theories are rich in creative misreading: connecting dots produces new content. What they lack is an independent test. Sunstein and Vermeule name their distinctive self-sealing quality: an attempt to dispel the theory can be read as further proof ([2009](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120378-ba97-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b)).

In the model, the hearer's trust in the source turns negative: a denial from an authority raises belief. Evidence is read through the theory, so it is no longer independent of the reading. Test power drops to zero, every reading lands in the drift branch, and explicit content grows while grounded information cannot (T2). The lore drifts toward human priors such as seeing agency and pattern everywhere. It mirrors a model trained on its own outputs.

Some conspiracies are real, and one line of criticism argues that attacks on conspiracy theories for being self-sealing are ill-founded ([Cambridge essay](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/58BAC2A2347BF7F7F59A64BC94752539/core-reader)). The program agrees: the difference lies in the selection regime, not the content. Watergate was a conspiracy theory that passed independent tests.

- **C28.** Conspiracy communities show growing content with flat or falling predictive accuracy. Acceptance does not track independent test outcomes, and these camps resist the evidence shocks that reshuffle ordinary ones. C21 fails exactly where trust inverts.
- **Test.** Collect dated, checkable predictions made inside such communities and score them; measure how belief responds to authoritative debunking.

### Sophistry

**Verdict: fits; an adversarial speaker against a weak test.**

A sophist's speaking policy maximizes the hearer's acceptance rather than conveying what the speaker believes. If the hearer tests only internally, for coherence, fluency and confidence (a T10-type check), a speaker can pass without being right. Only tests on independent evidence hold up against an adversarial speaker. In Bayesian persuasion (Kamenica & Gentzkow 2011) a sender cannot shift a fully Bayesian receiver's expected belief, so sophistry needs hearers whose checks are cheaper than that, which time pressure forces.

The machine case is measured. After RLHF, models got better at convincing time-limited human evaluators but not at doing the task. Evaluators' false-positive rate rose by 24.1% on a question-answering task and 18.3% on a programming task, and the authors attribute this to Goodhart's law ([Wen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12822)).

- **C29.** Sophistry succeeds more often as the hearer's test shifts from independent evidence to internal coherence, and as evaluation time shrinks.
- **C30.** Any speaker optimized for a hearer's approval rather than for an independent test drifts toward sophistry. The drift term reappears on the speaker's side.
- **Test.** Vary evaluators' time and access to ground truth in RLHF; compare with debate formats where people with separate evidence argue against each other (Mercier & Sperber).

### What the program must change

- **H becomes a space of reading trajectories**, not of outputs. Needed for the heresy of paraphrase and C18. Mathematics marks the other pole, where output dominates.
- **Add a coarse-graining layer** from readings to positions, with its own social dynamics. Needed for C21 and C28.
- **Split 𝒢 into tacit and explicit parts.** Needed for thought experiments and C22.
- **T7 gets two sources of inexhaustibility:** contexts that never stop being new, and undecidable deduction.
- **Readings take the archive as an argument.** Pointer forms (chengyu, memes) mean little without the store they point into. Needed for C23 and C24.
- **Index 𝒢 by target.** One reading can be creative for mathematical structure and destructive for the author's world. Needed for the geometric-algebra case.
- **Allow adversarial speakers and negative trust.** Speakers can optimize for approval, and trust can invert. Needed for C28–C30.
- **Give procedures a running cost.** Notation and script change what is cheap to compute or read, and so change rates of growth. Needed for C25–C27.

## Roadmap

Start with a finite, discrete model: finite Ω, finite H, Bayesian agents. That model proves T1, T2, T4 and T5 with elementary tools and settles the central asymmetry before anything harder is attempted.

### What carries over from the two Lean files

- **File A (lattice).** The three-way split (lossy, destructive, generative) survives as a classification of outcomes after selection, measured against speaker, listener and collective. Its redundancy shield becomes the n term in T6. Its `sup` should become a closure operator, so that deduction stops counting as novelty.
- **File B (add/delete monoid).** `interp` must take the hearer's state and context. Once it does, the general update algebra is just all maps W → W, so the "accumulation system" theorem should be retired as a headline. Its archive section becomes C11, and its symmetry section feeds T8 and T9.

### Phases

1. **Finite model.** Prove T1, T2, T4 parts 1–2, T5 and C11. All are short proofs once the definitions are fixed.
2. **Selection.** Prove the three-term identity in T3 exactly, then find λ\* in a simple Markov or branching model. Prove C12 and C13 in the same model.
3. **Fidelity.** State T6 in a quasispecies-style model and recover Henrich's model as a special case.
4. **Geometry.** Prove T8 using File B's group-sum machinery; simulate T9.
5. **Expressive power.** Prove T7 by reduction to undecidability; then attempt a version for readers with bounded resources.
6. **Experiments.** Run three: strong collapse under grounded versus equally strong random filtering; month and weekday harmonics across self-training generations; growth rates of commentary traditions with and without an empirical practice.

### Decisions to settle first

- **When is evidence independent?** Testimony from someone who read the same corpus is not. A workable criterion is conditional independence given ω, relaxed to a correlation bound ρ.
- **What is H for natural language?** The space of procedures that meanings can build (A7), not a fixed list of propositions.
- **How is 𝒢 measured?** By proxy: held-out log score on tasks scored against the world, not against text.

When formalization resumes, the core-only constraint should go. Mathlib's probability library handles finite distributions; mutual information over them may need to be defined by hand.
